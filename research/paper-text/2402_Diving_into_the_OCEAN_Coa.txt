Abstract
This paper introducesEviOCEAN, a multi-001
agent framework for personality detection002
based on Big Five. Different from prior work003
that directly maps input text to a trait, EviO-004
CEAN addresses two main concerns of LLM-005
based models: cognitive biases and the lack006
of informative evidence of personal traits. For007
cognitive biases, EviOCEAN comprises an ex-008
tractor that extracts seven psychological indi-009
cators formed a trait. For complementary ev-010
idence, it offers a coarse-to-fine manner for011
evidence validation. The validation uses a sum-012
marizer to retain informative information and013
high- and low-trait level disambiguation to pro-014
vide evidence for final prediction using deter-015
ministic routing. Experimental results on four016
datasets show that EviOCEAN improves perfor-017
mance personality detection and produces more018
balance scores in OCEAN classes. EviOCEAN019
is flexible and easy to adapt to any LLMs.020

2 Related Work106
Text-based personality detectionEarly meth-107
ods inferred personality from lexical, psycholin-108
guistic, and conversational features (Mairesse et al.,109
2007; Pennebaker and King, 1999). The methods110
range from using Transformer (Yang et al., 2021) or111
BERT (Kerz et al., 2022). Resources such as PAN- 112
DORA extended the task to large Reddit profiles 113
(Gjurkovi´c et al., 2021). However, these supervised 114
methods primarily associate textual representations 115
with labels, leaving supporting evidence outside 116
the explicit assessment target (Sun et al., 2024).117
LLM-based personality inferenceLLMs ex- 118
press personality knowledge and inference pro- 119
cedures directly through prompts, although fine- 120
grained distinctions remain difficult (V Ganesan 121
et al., 2023). The techniques range from prompting 122
LLMs with a questionnaire (PsyCoT) (Yang et al., 123
2023), zero-shot prompting (Lin et al., 2026), per- 124
sonality alignment (Zhu et al., 2025), or retrieved 125
examples (Li et al., 2025b). Relevance filtering 126
can further reduce unrelated information (Hofmann 127
et al., 2025). However, prompts may shift predic- 128
tions toward trait presence and produce class-wise 129
asymmetries (Di Cursi et al., 2025). These methods 130
provide psychological or evidence-based guidance, 131
but do not organize inference around the distinction 132
between temporary states and stable traits.133
PADO introduces a multi-agent framework that 134
contrasts levels (high and low) of each trait (Yeo 135
et al., 2025). EviOCEAN follows this multi- 136
perspective direction but distinguishes two major 137
differences. First, EviOCEAN provides evidence 138
in a coarse-to-fine manner by using a summarizer 139
to reduce noise and high- and low-trait level disam- 140
biguation that focuses on low-trait level samples. 141
Second, EviOCEAN integrates seven psycholog- 142
ical indicators in a non-induced way rather than 143
forcing LLMs to predict high and low trait levels.144

Following an evidence-centered view of personality159
judgment (Funder, 1995), we design EviOCEAN160
in Figure 2. Evi includes an evidence summarizer161

3.4 Indicator Extractor182
As mentioned, LLMs may exhibit a variety of cog-183
nitive biases on some OCEAN classes (Figures 1184
and 3). To mitigate these biases, we integrate social185
science into our framework. Psychological stud-186
ies mentioned that personality traits are manifested187
through recurring patterns formed by several as-188
pects and levels. The Whole Trait Theory stated189
that a trait includes a descriptive side and an ex- 190
planatory side (Fleeson and Jayawickreme, 2015; 191
Jayawickreme et al., 2019). The descriptive side 192
contains behavior, thought, emotion, and motiva- 193
tion states. The explanatory side consists of states 194
created from the social-cognitive mechanism such 195
as goals, beliefs, expectations, interpretations, mo- 196
tives, self-regulatory processes, perception of a situ- 197
ation. The personality systems framework (Mayer, 198
2015) organized personality into four functional 199
areas (the systems set): energy development (mo- 200
tives and emotions), knowledge guidance (mental 201
models, beliefs, and self-models), action implemen- 202
tation (plans, actions, and behavior), and executive 203
management (self-control, awareness and regula- 204
tion). The framework also considered personality 205
in the relation with external environment. A New 206
Big Five divided personality into four levels: dis- 207
positional traits, characteristic adaptations (goals, 208
motives, values, coping strategies, beliefs, and so- 209
cial roles), narrative identity, and culture and social 210
context (McAdams and Pals, 2006).211
Inspired by these studies, we design seven psy- 212
chological indicators in different levels that may 213
constitute a trait. They are emotions, cognition, 214
sociality, motivation/goals, behavior/habits, self- 215
concept/identity, and environment/context (detailed 216
description of indicators is shown in A.1). The ex- 217
tractor extracts the evidence (description) of each 218
indicator from the input by using prompts. Let 219
D denote a set of seven indicators, the extractor 220
returns one evidence record for each indicatori i,d.221

5 Results and Discussion349
5.1 Main Results350
Zero-shot comparisonTable 2 shows that EviO- 351
CEAN produces the best results on four datasets 352
in the zero-shot setting. The improvement comes 353
from two main aspects. First, Evi takes into ac- 354
count social science that includes seven psycho- 355
logical indicators to reduce the cognitive biases of 356
LLMs. The scores of Evi in OCEAN classes are 357
quite balanced, while the baseline that directly uses 358
LLMs is biased in some classes. For example, GPT- 359
4o focuses more on O and N classes on ESSAYS 360
and PANDORA while Solar is biased on O, E, and 361
A classes on My Personality. By integrating seven 362
psychological indicators, Evi can mitigate biases 363
and improve performance. Second, Evi offers a 364
coarse-to-fine manner to validate evidence using a 365
summarizer and an evidence disambiguation layer. 366
It provides fine-grained and informative informa- 367
tion. Combined with the psychological indicators, 368
Evi can make correct predictions. PADO follows 369
our EviOCEAN because it also incorporates social 370
science to the reasoning framework. The baseline 371
and PsyCoT using single prompts produce poor 372
results due to the shallow analysis of input, without 373
using social science or coarse-to-fine information 374

6 Conclusion483
This paper presentedEviOCEAN, a zero-shot 484
multi-agent framework, that addresses two con- 485
cerns of LLMs for OCEAN trait inference: cog- 486
nitive biases and lack of informative evidence of 487
OCEAN traits. To mitigate the cognitive biases of 488
LLMs, EviOCEAN integrates psychological indica- 489
tors in different levels and views from an indicator 490
extractor. To address the lack of clear evidence, 491
EviOCEAN uses a course-to-fine evidence valida- 492
tion process that combines information from the 493
extractor and an evidence summarizer for trait level 494
disambiguation. The final trait prediction is done 495
by deterministic routing and selective conflict ar- 496
bitration. Experimental results on four datasets 497
with four LLM backbones show that EviOCEAN 498
produces better performance than strong zero-shot 499
methods. Ablation studies also indicate more bal- 500
ance scores produced by the proposed method in 501
OCEAN classes than baselines. EviOCEAN offers 502
a flexible way to integrate social science into per- 503
sonality inference with any LLMs. Future work 504
will consider multilingual settings, additional text 505
genres, and more efficient evidence assessment.506

Limitations507
Although promising results have been achieved,508
the proposed method has the following limitations.509
First, the evaluation covers four primarily English-510
language datasets and several text genres, so fur-511
ther study is needed for multilingual and culturally512
varied settings. Second, the proposed method fol-513
lows the binary high/low labels provided by the514
selected benchmarks; continuous trait scores may515
offer a more detailed representation of personality.516
Finally, the multi-agent process requires more in-517
ference time and API calls than direct prompting,518
although the reported efficiency is more favorable519
than the larger PADO pipeline. We acknowledge520
this limitation because of higher latency and cost521
compared to direct prompting; however, a more522
sophisticated design is also encouraged.523
