Abstract001
This paper introduces CARE, a cognitive002
appraisal-gated multi-agent framework that003
uses appraisal not only as an emotion cue but004
also as a control signal for selective reason-005
ing. For each utterance, CARE extracts an006
eight-field appraisal state and applies theory-007
informed deterministic routing rules to six psy-008
chological and multimodal reasoning agents.009
Each activated agent produces a source-specific010
emotion prediction for Weighted Evidence V ot-011
ing that combines predictions from an always-012
active Text Agent and an appraisal-based emo-013
tion predictor. Experimental results on MELD014
and IEMOCAP show that CARE produces sig-015
nificant improvements over strong multi-agent016
methods in the zero-shot setting. Ablation stud-017
ies indicate that cognitive appraisal provides018
an effective routing method that offers fewer019
reasoning agents and a lower inference cost.020
1 Introduction021
Emotion recognition in conversation (ERC) is a022
context-sensitive NLP task that aims to identify023
the emotion expressed in each utterance within024
a conversation (Poria et al., 2019b). It serves as025
a foundational component of empathetic AI sys-026
tems in applications such as mental health sup-027
port, customer service, and education (Majumder028
et al., 2019; Pereira et al., 2025). However, most029
approaches have still predicted emotions directly030
from observable conversational evidence using uni-031
modalities (mainly text) or multimodalities (text,032
visual cues and speech) (Ghosal et al., 2019, 2020;033
Hu et al., 2021; Zhang et al., 2025; Lei et al., 2024;034
Ai et al., 2025; Lyu et al., 2026), while leaving an035
important intermediate implicit process: How does036
the speaker cognitively appraise the situation.037
Psychological theories offer complementary038
ways to explain how emotions arise (Moors, 2009;039
Edwards, 1999). Several theories have been applied040
to improve the reasoning of AI models such as Cog-041
nitive Behavioral Therapy (CBT) (Beck, 2020) for042
distortion detection (Chen et al., 2023), personality 043
traits for capturing relatively stable dispositional 044
tendencies (Roberts and DelVecchio, 2000; Watson 045
and Clark, 1992), or social reasoning for relational 046
meaning (Roberts and DelVecchio, 2000; Watson 047
and Clark, 1992), Recent studies have introduced 048
psychological grounding multi-agent frameworks 049
for ERC (Hotta et al., 2025; Nguyen et al., 2026). 050
However, applying all indicators to every utter- 051
ance may be unnecessary. Some utterances can 052
be recognized from surface context or multimodal 053
cues alone, while others require deeper reasoning 054
about subjective belief, social states or personal- 055
ity evidence (Ghosal et al., 2020; Xu et al., 2024; 056
Wang et al., 2024). This raises a question: Which 057
evidence should be considered? We argue that 058
cognitive appraisal provides a principal method for 059
this decision. It explains that emotions depend not 060
only on events or situations themselves, but also on 061
how individuals evaluate them in relation to their 062
goals, responsibility, perceived control, certainty, 063
novelty, and social norms (Lazarus, 1991; Smith 064
and Ellsworth, 1985; Scherer, 2001). Figure 1 illus- 065
trates this process using Rachel’s second turn. At 066
the surface level, the text-only agent predicts the 067
utterance as neutral due to no strong emotional sig- 068
nals. However, cognitive appraisal reveals a deeper 069
interpretation: the situation is highly relevant to 070
Rachel’s relational goal, involves low perceived 071
control and uncertainty, and signals possible loss or 072
separation. Guided by this, DoT and Social gates 073
are activated because of his subjective belief (re- 074
lational loss) and a need for reassurance. These 075
analyses indicate that the utterance expresses a neg- 076
ative emotion that aligns with sadness (Lazarus, 077
1991; Hofmann et al., 2020; Troiano et al., 2023). 078
To answer the question, we introduce CARE 079
(Cognitive Appraisal-gated Reasoning for Emotion 080
Recognition), a psychology-guided multi-agent 081
framework for MERC. CARE uses zero-shot 082
prompts to guide LLM agents to estimates how 083

2 Related Work112
Emotion Recognition in Conversations The113
wide range methods of ERC use conversational114
context, speaker interactions, and external knowl-115
edge (Majumder et al., 2019; Ghosal et al., 2020)116
or LLMs fine-tuning (Lei et al., 2024; Liu et al., 117
2024; Zhang et al., 2025; Fu et al., 2025). Re- 118
cently, agent-based methods further decompose 119
emotion reasoning into specialized roles that an- 120
alyze personality, cognitive distortions, and mul- 121
timodal evidence (Mozikov et al., 2024; Nguyen 122
et al., 2026). For multimodal ERC, existing meth- 123
ods integrated textual, acoustic and visual signals 124
using recurrent, graph-based, or Transformer archi- 125
tectures (Ma et al., 2023; Huang et al., 2024; Ai 126
et al., 2025; Wu et al., 2025a; Lyu et al., 2026). 127
Psychological Modeling for ERC Psychology- 128
grounded methods explain emotions through cog- 129
nitive and dispositional factors that are not di- 130
rectly observable from conversational utterances. 131
Appraisal-based studies modeled emotions through 132
evaluations of events (Troiano et al., 2023; Yeo and 133
Jaidka, 2025). CBT-inspired approaches used DoT 134
to separate objective facts from subjective inter- 135
pretations and identify cognitive distortions (Chen 136
et al., 2023; Hotta et al., 2025). Personality-based 137
methods capture stable or situation-dependent 138
speaker characteristics (Wang et al., 2024), while 139
recent multi-agent frameworks combine personal- 140
ity, cognitive distortions, and multimodal evidence 141
for emotion prediction (Nguyen et al., 2026). How- 142
ever, they applied a fixed set of psychological and 143
multimodal reasoning sources without determining 144
which perspective is relevant to each utterance. 145
We focus on reconstruction of the appraisal pro- 146
cess that uses a gating method for information dis- 147
tillation from appraisal extraction for each utter- 148
ance. Combined with an appraisal predictor and 149
weighted voting, CARE makes significant differ- 150
ences compared to InsideOut (Mozikov et al., 2024) 151
and CMTD (Nguyen et al., 2026). 152

3.4 Cognitive Appraisal Extraction Agent177
Cognitive appraisal theory shows that emotion de-178
pends not only on an event, but on how a person179
evaluates its significance for their goals, expecta-180
tions, and social concerns (Lazarus, 1991; Smith181
and Ellsworth, 1985; Scherer, 2001; Ellsworth and182
Scherer, 2003). CARE represents this intermediate183
process by estimating how the speaker appraises184
the situation underlying each target utterance.185
apt = A(Ct, ut, st), (2)186
where A is the Cognitive Appraisal Extraction187
Agent and apt contains eight fields (A.1). Goal rel-188
evance and goal congruence capture the situation’s189
motivational significance; agency, certainty, and190
novelty describe its perceived cause and predictabil-191
ity (Scherer and Moors, 2019); moral violation and192
social threat capture its normative and relational193
meaning (Ellsworth and Scherer, 2003); and affect194
intensity records the strength of the implied re-195
sponse without assigning an emotion label (Russell196
and Barrett, 1999). The extracted appraisal state197
serves two roles. The Appraisal Predictor maps 198
it to an appraisal-conditioned emotion prediction, 199
and Deterministic Appraisal Gating uses it to select 200
appropriate indicators to the target utterance. 201
3.5 Deterministic Appraisal Gating 202
Appraisal captures how the speaker evaluates a 203
situation, but it does not fully explain why that 204
evaluation arises. Instead of using all extracted 205
appraisal information that may introduce noise, 206
CARE projects appraisal information into four psy- 207
chological and two multimodal reasoning gates 208
(agents) as complementary projection evidence. 209
Let Rt denote at least medium goal relevance; 210
Bt: blocked or mixed goal congruence; and Ut: 211
low certainty. Let It, Nt, St, and Mt denote at least 212
medium affect intensity, novelty, social threat, and 213
moral violation, respectively. Jt denotes agency as- 214
signed to the self, another person, or both, whereas 215
Ot restricts agency to another person or shared 216
agency. Finally, EVt and EAt indicate the avail- 217
ability of visual and acoustic evidence, and Ht 218
indicates that the target speaker has at least four 219
preceding turns. With Qt = It ∨ Ut ∨ Nt ∨ St, the 220
six activation rules are as follows. 221
DoT gate Cognitive appraisal theory shows that 222
people first determine whether a situation is rel- 223
evant to their goals before evaluating its conse- 224
quences (Aue et al., 2007). Following Chen et al. 225
(2023), the DoT (Diagnosis-of-Thought) gate sep- 226
arates observable facts from subjective thoughts, 227
contrasts evidence for and against those thoughts, 228
and identifies underlying beliefs or expectations 229
(Beck, 2020). The DoT gate is activated only when 230
the situation is goal-relevant and at least one of 231
these cues is presented (detail is shown in A.10). 232
gDoTt = Rt ∧ (Bt ∨ Ut ∨ It) (3) 233
Distortion gate This gate determines whether 234
the interpretation exhibits a recognized cognitive 235
distortion (defined in A.2) that corresponds to dy- 236
namic states of emotions (Nguyen et al., 2026). 237
The gate is considered only when DoT is active. 238
gDistt = gDoTt ∧ (Ut ∨ Jt ∨ St ∨ Mt) (4) 239
Social gate captures relational and normative 240
meaning (blame, rejection, face threat, and norm vi- 241
olation), which can be relevant to emotions (Fiske 242
et al., 2002; Hareli and Parkinson, 2008; Russell 243
and Giner-Sorolla, 2011). The rule activates the 244

5 Results and Discussion352
5.1 Improvement over Base LLMs353
Baselines Text-only is the Text Agent in Figure354
1. InsideOut (Mozikov et al., 2024) designs five355
shallow agents for ERC. AER-LLM (Hong et al.,356
2025) uses textualized eGeMAPS features to es-357
timate an emotion distribution. SpeechCueLLM358
(Wu et al., 2025b) converts acoustic features into359
natural-language descriptions and incorporates360
them into a prompt. CoT is a single-agent base-361
line that uses CoT technique. CMTD (Nguyen362
et al., 2026) is the latest multi-agent framework363
that combines distortions and traits for MERC.364
Results Table 2 shows that CARE achieves sig-365
nificant improvements over strong baselines. The366
improvement comes from three aspects. First,367
CARE is a selective framework based on appraisal368
gating. It provides a way that the model can select369
appropriate multimodal indicators for each utter-370
ance that helps to reduce noise and inference cost371
(Table 10). Second, it combines psychological with372
multimodal (textual, visual and acoustic) indica- 373
tors for emotional reasoning. Psychological indica- 374
tors provide deeper evidence of emotions using ap- 375
praisal projection. Combined with shallow features 376
extracted from text, vision, and speech, CARE can 377
predict emotions based on selective evidence. Ta- 378
ble 4 shows the contribution of each component. 379
Finally, voting also offers soft alignment among 380
predictors that helps to avoid over-prediction of a 381
single predictor (output observation is in A.8). 382
CMTD is the second best model because it is a 383
multi-agent framework that combines distortion 384
and trait detection. The combination provides 385
deeper understanding of latent patterns formed an 386
emotion. However, CMTD is still behind CARE 387
with quite large gaps, thanks to the gating mech- 388
anism. CoT obtains quite competitive scores be- 389
cause it can simulate the reasoning process based 390
on prompting. Other methods produce quite low 391
results. A possible reason is that they only use 392
shallow information for emotion prediction. 393
5.2 Comparative Reference to SOTA Methods 394
We made a reference by comparing CARE to SOTA 395
fine-tuned methods. We did not claim that CARE 396
achieve the best results over SOTA fine-tuned meth- 397
ods due to different settings.AdaIGN models intra- 398
speaker and inter-speaker context dependencies us- 399
ing an adaptive interaction graph network (Tu et al., 400
2024). DE-GCN captures dialogue and event rela- 401
tions for MERC by a graph neural network (GNN) 402
(Ai et al., 2024). GS-MCC uses a graph-spectrum- 403
based collaborative learning framework (Ai et al., 404
2025). CSS combines Synergistic Polynomial Fu- 405
sion and Pareto Gradient Modulator to create a 406
couple representation (Lyu et al., 2026). 407
Table 3 shows that there exit gaps between 408
CARE and SOTA methods. For MELD, the per- 409
formance of CARE is behind GS-MCC by around 410
3.0% because GS-MCC is a complex GNN model 411
using Fourier graph operators and is fine-tuned 412
on MELD and IEMOCAP. On IEMOCAP the 413
strongest fine-tuned method is CSS, which is about 414
5.2% above CARE. On the other hand, our CARE 415
performs in the zero-shot setting, which is practical 416
in actual applications. Because CARE needs no 417
annotated training data, it adapts easily to new do- 418
mains, such as medical or psychological treatment, 419
where high-quality labels are scarce. Interestingly, 420
CARE produces better scores than two fine-tuned 421
methods: AdaIGN and DE-GCN. It confirms the 422
efficiency of our design based on appraisal gating. 423

7 Conclusion 543
We introduced CARE, a cognitive appraisal-gated 544
multi-agent framework for multimodal emotion 545
recognition in conversations (MERC). CARE uses 546
appraisal both as emotion evidence and as a control 547
signal to selectively activate relevant psychological 548
and multimodal reasoning agents. Experiments on 549
MELD and IEMOCAP show consistent improve- 550
ments over strong MERC baselines across diverse 551
LLM backbones. Ablation and routing analyses 552
further demonstrate that these gains arise from se- 553
lecting relevant evidence rather than indiscrimi- 554
nately activating more agents, while also reducing 555
unnecessary computation. These findings highlight 556
cognitive appraisal as an effective mechanism for 557
building more accurate, efficient, and interpretable 558
emotion-aware conversational systems. CARE of- 559
fers a flexible way to integrate psychological indi- 560
cators into MERC frameworks. Future work will 561
investigate other psychological indicators and align- 562
ment methods for text, image, and speech. 563

Limitations564
Despite its promising results, the framework has565
several limitations. First, all experiments were con-566
ducted on MELD and IEMOCAP, both of which are567
acted datasets derived from television sitcoms and568
scripted dialogues. Because acted emotional ex-569
pressions may differ from those occurring in spon-570
taneous, real-world conversations, the framework571
should be further evaluated on more diverse and572
naturalistic conversational datasets. Second, the573
framework adopts a middle-frame strategy, extract-574
ing a single representative image to capture the575
speaker’s visual cues. We acknowledge that this576
approach is computationally efficient and helps re-577
duce latency; however, relying on one static frame578
may overlook temporal facial dynamics and micro-579
expressions that are important for identifying short-580
lived emotions such as disgust and surprise. Future581
work should investigate video-based processing582
methods that can process both visual and acoustic583
features while carefully balancing recognition per-584
formance against computational cost and latency.585
In addition, the framework should also investigate586
physical cues from the human body, such as pos-587
ture, gestures, head movements, and body dynam-588
ics. These features can provide complementary589
information to facial, acoustic, and textual signals,590
particularly when facial expressions are ambiguous591
or partially obscured, and may therefore improve592
emotion recognition in natural conversational set-593
tings. Finally, some parts of psychological ground-594
ing in Section 6.3 use small-scale human evalua-595
tion. We acknowledge this setting (combined with596
LM judge) due to high cost of human annotation;597
however, actual applications may require a larger598
evaluation scale to assess psychological indicators.599
Ethics Statement600
The authors confirm that this study raises no ethi-601
cal concerns. We state that the distortion detection602
component and the overall framework are not in-603
tended to replace clinical screening, diagnosis, or604
mental healthcare treatment. Distortion detection is605
used solely as an intermediate step in the emotional606
reasoning process, and the framework may occa-607

However, these predictions are employed only for609

A.10 Psychological Rationale of the Gates1118
The DoT gate uses goal relevance together with1119
blockage, uncertainty, or affect intensity to iden-1120
tify appraisal conflicts requiring cognitive anal-1121
ysis (Aue et al., 2007). The Distortion gate de-1122
pends on DoT because distorted thinking can only1123
be examined after identifying a subjective inter-1124
pretation. Low certainty motivates predictive-1125
bias checks, human agency motivates attributional1126
checks, social threat motivates interpersonal-bias1127
checks, and moral violation motivates rigid-norm1128
checks (Milne et al., 2019). The Social gate maps1129
social threat, moral violation, and other-caused 1130
goal blockage to relational and normative reason- 1131
ing (Richman and Leary, 2009). The Trait gate 1132
requires repeated speaker history and a salient trait- 1133
relevant situation to avoid confusing momentary 1134
states with stable tendencies (Fleeson, 2001). Fi- 1135
nally, the Visual and Acoustic gates use Qt to iden- 1136
tify appraisals for which nonverbal evidence may 1137
resolve affective ambiguity (Gentsch et al., 2015). 1138
